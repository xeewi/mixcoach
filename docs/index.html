Your mix, in six zones

Spectrum analyzers draw curves. MixCoach compares your tracks with each other, tells you which one crowds which zone, then what to do about it.

Sub

20–60 Hz

The very deep vibration you feel in your belly more than you hear it.

Bass

60–250 Hz

The body and warmth of the kick and the bass.

Low mids

250–500 Hz

Where almost every instrument has energy, so everything piles up.

Mids

500–2000 Hz

The heart of instruments and vocals, the only thing a phone can really play.

High mids

2–6 kHz

The attack and presence of sounds; your ear is very sensitive here.

Highs / air

6–20 kHz

Brightness, cymbals, breath, “air”.

01WarningSubKick ↔ Sub Bass

The kick and the Sub Bass clash around 39 Hz

Both tracks are loud in the same band, at the same place in the mix. The kick keeps the low end: the Sub Bass makes room.

Why? When two sounds play in the same frequencies, your ear can't separate them anymore: they hide each other, like two people talking at the same time.

How to fix it in Ableton

  1. Sidechain: Compressor on the bass, Sidechain input = the kick track, Ratio 4:1, fast Attack.
  2. Otherwise: EQ Eight on the bass, a Bell around 39 Hz, −2 to −3 dB.

Install in 2 minutes

You need Ableton Live 12 (Suite, or Standard + Max for Live).

  1. Download MixCoach.zip and unzip it.
  2. Copy the MixCoach folder to ~/Music/Ableton/User Library/Presets/Audio Effects/Max Audio Effect/ Documents\Ableton\User Library\Presets\Audio Effects\Max Audio Effect\
  3. Optional, but magic: copy MixCoachAuto to ~/Music/Ableton/User Library/Remote Scripts/Documents\Ableton\User Library\Remote Scripts\, restart Live and pick MixCoachAuto as a Control Surface (Settings → Link, Tempo & MIDI). Sensors then add themselves.
  4. Otherwise, drop MixCoach Sensor on each track and MixCoach on the Master. Press play: you're done.
